# Lovenest signature concept

Chosen by a concept round: four directions were pitched (private circles, voice, creators, playful culture) and judged on three lenses (appeal, feasibility and safety, revenue and brand fit). Summarised here; the mockup is in docs/mockups/.

**Tagline:** Everyone, or just your nest.

**Pitch:** A kind public feed with your nest built in: post to everyone or just your people from the same box, and the nest side is locked so even Lovenest can't read it.

## Signature features

### Nests (with Hatch)  
*launch*

Every post can go to Everyone or to one of your nests: small, named, end-to-end encrypted circles of your people, posted from the same box and read in the same Home feed.

- **How it works:** The composer gets an audience pill: Everyone, or one of your nests ('Roommates', 'The girls', 'Family'). A nest is a named circle of 2 to 64 people who can see each other, joined by invite only. Invites from people you don't already chat with arrive in Requests. Under the hood a nest is an E2EE group conversation with a nest flag, so a nest post is an encrypted message of a new 'post' kind. Each post gets a fresh random key, wrapped for each current member with crypto_box, which is exactly what routes/chats.ts and encryptMessage already do. Photos use the existing conversation-scoped encrypted media (media.ts), so only members get download URLs. Home merges public posts and nest posts on the phone, and nest posts sit in a rose frame with the nest's name and a lock. Replies under a nest post are encrypted messages with replyTo. Each nest is also a group chat in Messages: posts show in Home and chatter stays in the chat. A plaintext lane flag ('post' or 'chat') lets Home page through posts without decrypting the chat, and it is the only new metadata. Someone added later starts fresh, because older posts were never wrapped for them. Any public post can be shared into a nest as an ordinary encrypted message. Hatch arrives about two weeks after launch. On your own nest post, 'Hatch' re-posts your text and photos to Everyone; the phone re-uploads the photos unencrypted to the public posts/ prefix. The public post gets a small 'hatched · born in a nest 2 days ago' mark. The nest gets an encrypted 'It's out!' note on the original, with an optional one-tap first heart. Nest replies never travel with it.
- **Why it beats Threads:** Threads gives a post one audience. Its Buddy Lists were only a reported test in June 2026, and Meta has said Threads messaging won't be end-to-end encrypted (recon N3), so any private layer there would sit where Meta can read and rank it. Twitter Circle showed the danger: in April 2023 a bug pushed Circle posts into strangers' For You feeds, and X shut Circle down that October. On Lovenest a ranking or caching bug can't leak a nest post, because the server only holds ciphertext. Nests are several named, mutual circles rather than one close-friends list. They also fix cold start: your friends' posts make Home worth opening before the public network has filled up. Market it as 'a finsta built in', not as a first, because Instagram Close Friends and Snapchat Private Stories already exist.
- **Money:** Never paywalled: posting to nests, the number of nests and their size are all free. Plus adds cosmetics that every member sees: nest covers, colour themes and custom nest icons. The real return is retention and invites. A nest of 6 friends can pull in up to 5 new people at once, and each arrives with a reason to stay.
- **Guardrails:** Invite-only and capped at 64. No nest search, discovery or directory, and no paid nests, ever. Before launch, replace free-text reports from encrypted spaces with key-reveal reporting. The reporter sends the message id and its message key, the server decrypts its own stored ciphertext, and the moderator sees exactly what was sent, so nobody can invent a nest post to get someone banned. Members can report the nest, block, and leave silently. Removal is shown quietly ('you're no longer in this nest'). The composer says 'Only members can read this. They can still screenshot.' Hatch works only on your own posts. Before hatching photos it asks: 'This might show people from your nest. Check with them first.' First hearts are optional, with no list of who hasn't hearted and no extra weight in ranking. Copy claims only what the crypto guarantees, such as 'only members can read nest posts', and never 'not even a court order'.
- **Build:** M, about 1.5–2 weeks after the follows/Home pivot, plus S (3–4 days) for Hatch. Server: a nest flag on conversations and a lane column on messages (schema.ts); GET /nests/feed?before= over messages joined to members, with the joined_at and cleared_at rules; hatched_at on posts. Crypto: 'post' and 'hatched' MessageBody kinds, plus tests. Mobile: audience pill in compose.tsx, NestPostCard, merge into lib/useFeed.ts, a reply sheet, and a create-nest flow reusing new-group.tsx. Prerequisites: key-reveal reporting (S–M) and roadmap 1.7 (unknown-kind fallback and version gate).

### Tonight's Question (with Split nights)  
*launch*

One kind question lands every evening. Answer it to everyone or to a nest, or pass, and you get to see what your people said.

- **How it works:** At 7 pm in your own time zone, a question card sits at the top of Home. Lovenest writes the questions ('What small thing made today okay?', 'What song plays when you walk into a room?'). A person reviews user suggestions, and the ones that go live are credited ('asked by @mia'). You answer in up to 280 characters or one photo, either to Everyone or to one of your nests. A public answer is a post tagged to the question (posts.prompt_id). A nest answer is encrypted with the Status envelope for that nest's current members (encryptFor('prompt:<clientId>'), one wrapped key each). When answering to a nest you can also send a voice note with the existing chat recorder. Until you answer or tap 'Pass tonight', friends' answers are blurred bubbles with their avatars. The server holds back your copy of their wrapped keys until it has recorded that you answered or passed. That is a rule we keep, not cryptography, and the help text says so. Then the answers appear, followed by a strip of public answers people loved. About two nights a week it's a Split night: a this-or-that question ('Sunrise or sunset?', 'Call or text?') shown as two big halves. After you pick, you see the split for Everyone and for your nests, and which friends picked your side. After 10 shared Splits, a mutual's profile shows 'Taste twins · you agree on 18 of 24'. Every answer has 'Share as card': the card is rendered on the phone with react-native-view-shot, shared through expo-sharing, and links to lovenest.app/q/<date>, a web page with an OG image and the sharer's invite code. There are no streaks or counts. Answers collect in a private 'Your answers' archive, and on 31 December you get a 'Your year of tonights' card.
- **Why it beats Threads:** Threads has no shared moment that everyone takes part in. It suggests topics in the composer, and its ranking has rewarded rage bait (recon N57: one experiment drew 3,000+ replies). Tonight's Question gives lurkers something easy and kind to say every day, and getting lurkers to post is the hardest cold-start problem for a new social app. Answer-to-reveal is proven by BeReal and Paired, so the mechanic isn't new. The new parts are text answers that go public or into an encrypted nest, and Split nights that show which friends are your taste twins. It is also the app's only daily ritual: Daily Hum and the Split of the day fold into it, so Lovenest never stacks rituals.
- **Money:** Free forever, because it's the retention engine. Plus adds extra answer-card and year-card designs, plus private question decks you can run in your own nests or with your person, which fits the couples heritage. Chatting and answering stay free.
- **Guardrails:** Curated questions only: nothing about grief, bodies, money, politics or religion, and every user suggestion is reviewed before it goes live. 'Pass tonight' is as big as 'Answer'. Nobody sees who didn't answer, and there are no streaks, no counts and no 'you missed it'. The only notification is an optional quiet 8 pm reminder, which counts against an app-wide budget of one non-message push a day. Public answers are moderated like posts, and nest answers are reported with key-reveal reports. Split picks are stored like likes; they aren't encrypted, and the screen says so. They are shown only to mutuals who also voted, and you can hide them. Taste twins is one-to-one between mutuals, can be switched off, and is never a leaderboard or 'best friends' list. Split questions never ask about sensitive opinions. Time zone is declared as approximate location in Data safety.
- **Build:** M overall, plus S for Split nights. Server: routes/prompts.ts; prompts (local_date, text, kind, options, credit_user_id) and prompt_answers (user_id, local_date, mode, choice, sealed_ref); posts.prompt_id; users.time_zone with the 4 am wall-clock day helper from the streak spec. No scheduler is needed, because the phone works out 'tonight'. Taste twins is one SQL join over two users' choices. Mobile: QuestionCard, an answer sheet built from compose.tsx, a reveal list using PostCard and the Status decrypt helpers, and share cards (react-native-view-shot plus expo-sharing, one new EAS build). Web: a /q/:date page and OG image in routes/pages.ts. Content: about 120 questions written and reviewed before launch, then roughly one a day.

### Cozy Mode (with Lights out)  
*launch*

A one-tap vibe dial that makes your feed gentle and light on news, and a feed that kindly ends at your bedtime.

- **How it works:** A small dial sits at the top of Home with two settings at launch, Cozy and Everything. A third, Curious, comes later and adds learning and discussion without breaking news. Cozy hides posts that link to news sites or match a maintained list of news, politics and crisis words, and it favours posts the author tagged cozy or win. Authors can tag a post's vibe with one optional tap: cozy, win, deep, help or vent. Vent posts are folded for everyone behind 'Someone's having a rough day. Open?' so nobody is ambushed, with support resources one tap away. Every hidden post can be shown again through 'Why hidden?'. Lights out: you pick a bedtime, and after it Home switches to the plum night theme (theme.ts already has it) and Cozy only. After 20 posts the feed ends with 'That's everything for tonight. Sleep well.', with Tonight's Question as the last card if you haven't answered it. The dial and bedtime live on the phone (expo-secure-store). The app sends ?vibe=cozy with each feed request and the server doesn't store it, so no mood profile exists. News-link and hot-word flags are computed once, when a post is written.
- **Why it beats Threads:** Threads has a political-content setting, topic tuning and, since late 2025, a daily limit and sleep mode. Those are switches buried in settings, and the time limit locks you out. Cozy Mode is a visible mood choice in the feed itself, uses vibes the authors declare, and ends the scroll with a goodnight instead of a wall. It answers the recon's complaints about a random, stale feed and rage bait (recon #3 and #5), and it is the clearest proof of 'a kinder place to talk'. To be honest, the edge is in framing and feel, not a capability Threads lacks.
- **Money:** None, and never sold: wellbeing isn't a perk. Indirectly it makes Plus credible as a way to support an ad-free app, and the bedtime card is the natural store screenshot and word-of-mouth line ('the app that tells me to go to sleep').
- **Guardrails:** Per-viewer and opt-in. It never down-ranks anyone in other people's feeds, and it classifies posts, never people. Every hide can be undone and shows its reason. The word lists leave out identity and health terms, so people talking about their own lives (illness, layoffs, who they are) aren't filtered out as 'politics'. Promise 'fewer news posts', never 'no news'. The end card never locks you out: a small 'Keep scrolling' link stays. Vent folds come with support resources. No medical claims about sleep. Cozy Mode, like every safety and wellbeing control, never goes into Plus.
- **Build:** M. Server: posts.vibe (nullable enum) set in POST /posts; posts.flags (has_news_link, has_hot_words) computed at write time from a domain list and word lists in a new lib; a vibe filter param on feed() in routes/timeline.ts. Mobile: the dial on Home, vibe chips in compose.tsx, the folded vent cover in PostCard, and the bedtime setting and end card. Upkeep: review the lists monthly and localise them for each launch market.

### Group Hug  
*soon after launch*

Start a card for a friend's birthday or big day. Everyone signs it with a note, sticker or doodle, even from a web link, and it opens for them on the day.

- **How it works:** Start one from a friend's profile, from a 'Birthdays this week' row on Home, or from inside a nest. Birthdays are opt-in, month and day only, and shown to mutuals. Pick an occasion (birthday, congrats, get well, farewell, just because) and a delivery time, and you get a signing link to drop into any group chat: a nest, WhatsApp or Discord. People on Lovenest sign in the app. Anyone else signs on the web page with a name, a note of up to 140 characters, a sticker or a finger doodle, with no install needed. At the end they're asked to get the app ('Get Lovenest to see Ana open it'), and their signatures wait for the organizer's approval. Signatures land on a yearbook-style page at slight angles. The honoree sees nothing until delivery, then gets an unwrapping animation and the whole card. They choose to keep it private, pin it to their profile, or share it as a card through the same share-card pipeline as Tonight's Question. Delivery runs on a new per-minute opener in apps/server/src/index.ts, plus a check on read. Doodles are stored as bounded arrays of numeric points and rasterised by a strict renderer, never as raw SVG. v1 cards are readable by the server like posts, so they can be moderated. A private, end-to-end encrypted version signed inside a nest comes later.
- **Why it beats Threads:** Threads has nothing for celebrating one person; a birthday there is just a pile of replies. A signed card is the internet's kindest format and makes 'a kinder place to talk' visible. Facebook's 2019 Birthday Stories were separate stories, not one signed object. Group-card sites like Kudoboard charge per board, feel like office tools and sit outside your friends' graph. Group Hug is also the strongest invite loop here: the link travels through group chats people already use, 5 to 20 people arrive at a happy moment, and the honoree almost always shares the card.
- **Money:** Starting, signing and receiving are free. Plus adds animated covers, confetti, and pen and paper styles, which everyone sees. Later there could be a printed card mailed to the honoree as a one-off physical product. Assumption: about $8.99, with a few dollars of margin after the print vendor and postage. Physical goods may be sold outside Play Billing, but check Play's policy, vendor costs and how postal addresses are handled first.
- **Guardrails:** In-app signers must follow the honoree. Web signatures need the organizer's approval and are rate-limited per link. The honoree can decline, hide or report a card and turn off 'cards about me'. The organizer can remove any signature, and every signature can be reported. Delivered public cards are moderated like posts. Doodles are numeric points with size caps, rendered strictly. Birthdays are opt-in, have no year, are never listed publicly, can be deleted, and go into Data safety. Web signers' names go into the privacy policy. There are no 'who hasn't signed yet' nags, and signers get at most one reminder. The app stays 18+, and cards are never marketed to teens.
- **Build:** M (about 2–3 weeks) for cards signed in the app and on the web. The private E2EE variant is another M, later. Server: routes/cards.ts; cards (honoree_id, organizer_id, occasion, deliver_at, visibility, state) and card_signatures (text, sticker_id, doodle points capped at about 20 KB, layout seed, approved); signing-link codes; users.birthday_md; the per-minute opener, shared with Open When; a web signing page and OG image in routes/pages.ts. Mobile: a drawing canvas on react-native-svg and gesture-handler (both already installed), a seeded card layout, a Reanimated unwrap, and the birthdays row. Needs follows (for mutuals), Activity and working push.

### Open When (sealed posts and letters)  
*soon after launch*

Seal a post or a letter until a date you pick. Public seals sit in the feed showing their open date, and letters to a friend or a nest stay end-to-end encrypted until they open.

- **How it works:** In the composer, 'Seal until…' offers tomorrow morning, New Year's, a friend's birthday, one year from tonight, or any date: up to 1 year free, up to 10 years with Plus. A sealed post to Everyone appears straight away on your profile and in followers' feeds as a wax-sealed card. The card shows a preview line you write ('my predictions for 2027'), the open date, and an 'I'll be there' button that sends one notice when it opens. A sealed post can't be edited. Withdrawing it before the date leaves a visible 'withdrawn before opening' tombstone. On the day it becomes a normal post marked 'sealed 365 days ago', and your profile's Capsules tab lists every opened seal. The server can read sealed posts and checks them when they're created, so the card says 'sealed, not secret'. A sealed letter goes to yourself, one friend or a nest, encrypted with the Status envelope (encryptFor('letter:<clientId>', recipients)). The server stores the ciphertext and releases each recipient's wrapped key only at the open date. It can't read the letter, but it could in principle release one early, so we state that as a promise rather than a guarantee. Group capsules let everyone in a nest add a line or photo before a lock date, and nobody sees the others' additions until it opens. Openings run on the same per-minute opener as Group Hug, with a lazy check on read. Later, letters can move to drand timelock encryption (tlock-js), so that nobody, Lovenest included, can open them early.
- **Why it beats Threads:** Threads can schedule a post, but a scheduled post is invisible until it goes out, stays editable and proves nothing. A seal is visible from day one with its date and can't be changed, so 'I called it' is real, and openings become events: New Year's, birthdays, 'open when we graduate'. Letters are end-to-end encrypted, which Threads messages aren't. Open When also delivers the 'sealed love letters released on a date' that replica/launch/pricing.md already names as future Plus substance.
- **Money:** Free: seals and letters up to 1 year, with text and one photo or voice note. Plus: open dates up to 10 years, wax-seal and paper designs, and an 'opening party' theme for group capsules that everyone in the capsule sees. This is the real Plus substance pricing.md was waiting for before any price rise.
- **Guardrails:** Public seals are checked when they're created, which is why they stay readable by the server: no one can use them to publish unmoderated doxxing later. Each person can have at most 10 unopened seals, sealing is rate-limited, and letters are cancelled on block or account deletion. Recipients can pause deliveries, and there's a written policy for letters from someone who has died. Countdowns stay anticipatory and never frame waiting as loss. There are no 'only 2 hours left' pushes: one notice goes out at opening, only to people who chose 'I'll be there', and the waiting count sits under the hide-counts setting. No betting or prediction-market mechanics. The privacy policy covers storing letters for up to 10 years. Never claim 'even we can't open it' until tlock ships, and then name the drand trust assumption.
- **Build:** M, about 2 weeks. The tlock upgrade is another M, after a 1–2 day spike on Hermes. Server: posts.opens_at with an index, and an opens_at <= now filter in feed(), visiblePost() and the profile query in routes/timeline.ts; the preview line; post_waiters; sealed_letters, cloned from statuses (ciphertext, keys jsonb, opens_at, target); the tombstone state; a cap of 10 unopened seals per user; the shared opener. Mobile: a date picker and seal toggle in compose.tsx, a SealedCard variant in PostCard, a letter bubble in chat, and the Capsules tab.

### Backstory  
*later*

A public post with a sealed back side that only one of your nests can open: what you posted and what actually happened, in one post.

- **How it works:** Under the public text, the composer offers 'Add a backstory for…', and you pick one nest. Everyone sees the front: 'Got the job!!' and the nice photo. Members of that nest see the same card with a rose folded corner, and tapping it flips the card to the inside: the real story, the chaotic photo, the context only friends get. The inside is stored on the post row as ciphertext with one wrapped key per nest member, copying the Status pattern. encryptFor binds it to 'post:<clientId>', so the server can't move it onto another post. The feed returns the inner fields only to viewers a key was wrapped for, so nobody else even learns a backstory exists. Inside photos are encrypted under a new post-inner/<author>/<clientId>/ media prefix and handed only to key holders, like status media. Deleting or removing the front deletes the back.
- **Why it beats Threads:** Threads, Buddy Lists included, gives a post one audience. Showing different versions to different people takes two posts or two accounts, which is why finstas exist. Backstory folds that habit into one post, and because the inside is end-to-end encrypted, no recommendation system or bug can surface it. It's the feature people will use to explain Lovenest in one line. We found no social app with a per-post encrypted inner layer. That isn't proof none exists, so don't market it as a first.
- **Money:** Nothing directly. Later, Plus could add fold styles and reveal animations that every member sees. Its value is word of mouth and a reason for friends to join, because they have to be on Lovenest to read the back.
- **Guardrails:** Ship it only when nests are busy (for example, once most weekly actives are in a nest of 3 or more people), and only once key-reveal reporting covers backstories. The reporter sends the post id and inner key, the server decrypts its stored ciphertext, and an abusive inside gets the whole post removed. If the inside @mentions someone outside the nest, the phone asks gently: 'Ben can't see this side. Would you say it to him?' The composer says 'Your nest can still screenshot.' The optional 'there's more for close friends' tease is off by default and shows no counts. Strangers never learn a backstory exists unless the author turns the tease on.
- **Build:** M, about 1 week once Nests exist. Server: inner_nonce, inner_ciphertext and inner_keys (jsonb) on posts, with the same zod shapes as POST /status; per-viewer filtering (the statusFor pattern in routes/status.ts); a post-inner media prefix check in media.ts; a check that the recipients are exactly a current nest's members (the 409 'contacts changed' pattern). Mobile: a backstory section in compose.tsx, encryptFor with maxRecipients 512 (lib/session.tsx), a Reanimated flip in PostCard.tsx, and session.decrypt.

## How Lovenest earns (no ads)

No ads, no data sales and no paid reach, ever. Lovenest earns from one subscription, Lovenest Plus ($2.99/month, $19.99/year), and later from two small extras.

What Plus holds (cosmetics and real substance, all visible to everyone they touch):
- Open When: open dates up to 10 years, plus seal and paper designs.
- Nests: covers, themes and icons.
- Group Hug: covers, confetti and pen styles.
- Tonight's Question: answer-card and year-card designs, plus private question decks for nests or couples.
- The pricing.md v1 extras: heart styles, themes, app icons, 7-day status and a supporter badge.

Never in Plus: posting, following, messaging, nests or their size, Cozy Mode, safety, recovery or encryption.

Numbers (all assumptions, built on replica/launch/pricing.md):
- After Play's 15%, net is $2.54/month or $16.99/year. With 60% of subscribers on yearly, the blended net is about $1.87 per subscriber per month.
- pricing.md's baseline is 0.9% of Android downloads paying by day 35 (RevenueCat 2026), and optional cosmetic tiers do worse. With the substance above, assume 0.5–2% of monthly actives.
- Running costs are about $0.03–0.05 per active user per month (pricing.md's rate). A public app with public media, web pages and moderation sits at the top of that range.

What that gives:
- 10k monthly actives: 50–200 subscribers, about $90–370 a month, against roughly $300–500 of costs. Plus does not pay for the servers.
- 100k: 500–2,000 subscribers, about $0.9k–3.7k a month, against roughly $3k–5k. Near break-even at best.
- 1M: about $9k–37k a month. At today's per-user rate costs would be $30k–50k, so profit needs cost per user to fall with scale and conversion near the top of the range.

Extras:
1. Founding lifetime Plus at $39.99, capped at the first 500 buyers (already in pricing.md). That's at most about $17k once ($33.99 net each), and only if it sells out.
2. Group Hug printed and mailed cards, later. Suppose 2% of delivered cards get printed at about $8.99 with about $3 margin. Then 100k actives sending about 3,000 cards a month adds roughly $180 a month. It's small, but it may sit outside Play's fee because it's a physical good; check Play's policy first.
3. Creator money, starting with Keepsakes: a 10% published cut, with receipts that show who got what. Do this only once creators with audiences show up. The creator concept estimates about $130 a month at 10k users and $1.3k a month at 100k, after 3–4 weeks of Play Billing, Stripe, ledger and tax plumbing.

Decision needed: pricing.md defines Plus as one plan covering you and your person. Social apps price per person (Discord Nitro Basic, Snapchat+, Telegram Premium). The numbers above assume per-person pricing, and covering two people would roughly halve them. Keep a 'Plus for two' option as the couples-heritage variant if you want it.

Honest conclusion: a public Lovenest can out-earn the private messenger only by having many more users, not by earning more per user. Revenue stays around $0.01–0.04 per active user a month, and public posting adds moderation, storage and web costs. The signature features mostly earn indirectly, through retention and invites that grow the user base Plus depends on. Until about 100k actives, treat Plus as the way people support an ad-free app, and keep costs low: resize photos, cap voice notes, keep public media small, use the free TURN tier.

## Honest risks

- Launch scope: the launch blockers (push, TURN, calls ringing in the background) and the core pivot (follows, replies as posts, Activity; recon size L, about a quarter) come first. The three launch features add roughly 5–7 weeks on top: Nests plus key-reveal reporting, Tonight's Question with share cards, and Cozy Mode, each about M. That's an estimate, not a measurement. If time runs short, launch with Tonight's Question and Cozy Mode and ship Nests two weeks later; until then Tonight's Question offers Everyone only.
- Cold start: Nests, nest answers, Group Hug, taste twins and Backstory all need a person's friends to be on Lovenest, and the app starts empty. Their invite paths are the growth plan: nest invites, Group Hug web signing, and share cards carrying invite codes. Tonight's public strip and the Cozy feed have to feel good with strangers alone.
- Moderating what we can't read: nests, nest answers, letters and backstories are end-to-end encrypted, so moderators only see what members report. Today a report is free text (reports.details in routes/safety.ts), so anyone can fake one; key-reveal reporting must ship before Nests. Play's UGC and child-safety policies and the EU DSA still apply. Keep nests invite-only, capped at 64, with no discovery and no money.
- Kindness tension: a private layer makes it easier to be unkind out of sight. Backstory's polite front with a mocking back, or nest chatter about public posters, could get Lovenest framed in the press as having a 'secret gossip layer'. That is why Psst is cut, Backstory waits for busy nests, and its mitigations (whole-post removal, the mention nudge, screenshot warning) ship in v1.
- Encryption doesn't sell to 18–30s, and it raises the cost of losing your key. Today a forgotten password loses all encrypted history, because sign-in unwraps the key backup only with the password-derived key (lib/session.tsx). Nests put more of people's real lives behind that key. A recovery path (Spare Key or similar, after email verification and an external review) has to follow. Keys are also static, with no forward secrecy. Marketing should lead with the social mechanic and claim only what the crypto guarantees.
- Naming: 'nest' must mean only the encrypted circles. The streak badge in docs/roadmap.md (2.2: 'a nest with an egg', still an open decision at roadmap:157) needs a new icon. The concepts' other uses are dropped or renamed: 'My nest' for contacts, twigs, 'Nest of the year', Inner Nest and 'That's the nest for tonight'. Hatch keeps the egg.
- Infrastructure gaps: there is no job runner in apps/server/src/index.ts, and Group Hug and Open When both need the per-minute opener. The realtime hub and rate limiter run in a single process, so they break once Railway runs more than one instance. New encrypted message kinds ('post', 'hatched', letters) need roadmap 1.7's unknown-kind fallback and version gate, or older builds show blanks.
- Novelty and content fatigue: daily prompts and friend polls fade. Discord shut down Gas nine months after buying it, and BeReal suffered alarm fatigue. Tonight's Question needs a steady supply of good questions, about 365 a year, reviewed for each language. Keep it the only daily ritual, with one non-message push a day across the whole app.
- Prior art: Instagram Close Friends, Snapchat Private Stories, BeReal, Kudoboard, FutureMe, and Threads' own political-content setting and sleep mode already exist. Lovenest's edge is the combination plus the encryption, so never market any single feature as a first.
- Money: Plus won't pay for the servers below roughly 100k actives, and a public app costs more to run than a private messenger because of moderation, public media and web pages. The founder's bet that a public app earns more only pays off at scale.
- Privacy and store paperwork: Data safety and the privacy policy must change for birthdays, time zones (approximate location), web signers' names, letters stored for up to 10 years, and the share-card web pages. Launching Plus means changing 'No in-app purchases' in apps/server/src/routes/pages.ts:215 and docs/play-store/README.md:17. The 18+ gate is only a self-declared checkbox.
- Couples heritage: a controlling partner could pressure someone to add them to every nest or to show their taste twins. Keep leaving and changing nests silent, keep taste twins easy to switch off, and tie both into the roadmap's account-safety work (3.4).

## Considered and rejected

- **Psst (side talk on public posts):** No judge put it in their top 5. Its 'friends as the algorithm' idea overlaps Instagram Blend (Apr 2025) and the Reels Friends tab (Aug 2025). Next to Backstory, it would make the private layer read as a gossip layer, and the claim that the server can't link a nest to a post is overstated, because post fetches right after nest messages leak timing. Nests keeps a plain 'share to a nest' action. Revisit the avatar overlay only once nests are busy.
- **Sealed (drand timelock posts):** Merged into Open When. Its server-held seal ships first; tombstones and the Capsules tab come from Sealed, and tlock is the later upgrade for letters. Public capsules that open by themselves without moderation would be a dead-man's-switch doxxing tool, and tlock on Hermes is unproven.
- **Hatch (as its own feature):** Folded into Nests as an action on your own nest posts. It's cheap (S) and on brand, but it scored 4 on appeal on its own and only makes sense once nests exist.
- **Daily Hum:** Merged into Tonight's Question, which allows a voice-note answer when answering to a nest. A public daily voice ritual means moderating answers by ear every night, and a second daily ritual would break the one-ritual, one-push budget.
- **Splits (as their own feature):** Folded into Tonight's Question as Split nights with taste twins. That keeps the most shareable card (appeal 8) without a second daily ritual or the full poll builder. The no-account web 'Take my 10 Splits' page waits until later, and its votes never count in totals.
- **Spare Key:** A real need, but trust infrastructure rather than a signature feature: nobody opens the app or invites a friend for account recovery. It's safety-critical crypto. It needs email verification (roadmap 3.5), a fingerprint check so the server can't swap in its own key, a real random-number source on Hermes, and an external review. Build it as infrastructure after launch.
- **Pass the Pen:** Warm and buildable (feasibility 7), but it appeals mostly to people who like writing. It brings co-author moderation rules and another encrypted message kind. Revisit it as a format once the six signatures prove out.
- **Out Loud (voice posts and 'Out loud first' replies):** Threads and X already have voice posts. Public voice-first apps (Clubhouse, Airchat) spiked and faded. Captions are edited by the author, so abusive audio can hide under a clean caption. A voice-only reply rule cuts replies and excludes people who can't use it.
- **Chorus (voice reactions):** Its 2-second public voice clips are a cheap abuse channel that captions won't catch, and audio doesn't screenshot. Six Lovenest-made Chimes could come back later as Plus sound packs.
- **Around (presence and waves):** An empty presence row at launch tells people 'nobody's here'. Presence is a stalking and coercive-control surface, which matters given the couples heritage, and sending messages would leak 'invisible' status. The realtime hub is also single-process.
- **Porch (drop-in voice):** An L build gated on TURN and background calling, which are unbuilt launch blockers. Live E2EE audio can't be moderated. Discord voice channels and WhatsApp Voice Chats already cover it, and social audio tends to fade.
- **Keepsakes:** The best creator-money idea, but premature. There are no creators with audiences to tip, and it needs 3–4 weeks of Play Billing, ledger, Stripe and tax plumbing. Play doesn't support donations, so it needs a policy check, and serial-numbered scarcity drifts toward NFT-style spend-for-status. Revisit as the first creator feature once creators show up, with open-books receipts.
- **Inner Nest:** A paid E2EE room of strangers contradicts the 'no paid nests with strangers' rule. It's unmoderatable, attracts adult content in an 18+ app, and reuses 'nest' for a fourth meaning.
- **Ask & Listen:** 'Ask credit' earned from other people's purchases is a quasi-currency that bends the no-coins rule. Paying askers when others listen rewards provocative questions, Fenda itself boomed and faded, and it needs automatic refunds and a scheduler.
- **Kindshare:** It needs a base of Plus subscribers and the full payout plumbing. It takes 30% of Plus revenue at a size where Plus doesn't cover the servers, and below Stripe's $50 minimum almost no creator would ever be paid. Revisit at scale.
- **Keepers' Share:** It needs all the money plumbing plus public communities, which the recon parks. Paying volunteer moderators raises tax and conflict-of-interest problems over tiny sums. Keep only its open-books receipt line for later creator money.
